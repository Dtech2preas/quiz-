SUBTOPIC 1: MOMENTUM AND IMPULSE

Definitions & Principles:
- Momentum (p): The product of an object's mass and its velocity. (p = mv). It is a vector quantity. Unit: kg·m·s⁻¹
- Newton's Second Law in terms of Momentum: The net (or resultant) force acting on an object is equal to the rate of change of momentum of the object in the direction of the net force. (F_net = Δp / Δt).
- Impulse (J): The product of the net force acting on an object and the time the net force acts on the object. (J = F_net * Δt = Δp). Unit: N·s or kg·m·s⁻¹.
- Isolated System: A system on which the net external force is zero.
- Principle of Conservation of Linear Momentum: The total linear momentum of an isolated system remains constant (is conserved) in magnitude and direction. (Σp_initial = Σp_final).
- Elastic Collision: A collision in which both total momentum and total kinetic energy are conserved. (ΣE_k_initial = ΣE_k_final).
- Inelastic Collision: A collision during which kinetic energy is not conserved (some kinetic energy is converted to heat, sound, or deformation). Momentum is still conserved.

Common Distractors / Misconceptions to use in `wrong_answers_pool`:
- Defining momentum as a scalar instead of a vector.
- Confusing the units of impulse (N·s) with Force (N) or Work (J).
- In calculations, failing to assign a negative sign to a change in direction (e.g., bouncing balls). If a ball hits a wall at 5 m/s right and bounces back at 4 m/s left, Δv = -4 - (+5) = -9 m/s. Students often mistakenly use Δv = 5 - 4 = 1 m/s. Calculate this wrong result and use it as a distractor!
- Assuming ALL collisions conserve kinetic energy.
- Forgetting to add masses together in perfectly inelastic collisions (where objects stick together: m1v1 + m2v2 = (m1+m2)v_final).

SUBTOPIC 2: VERTICAL PROJECTILE MOTION IN 1D

Definitions & Principles:
- Projectile: An object upon which the only force acting is the force of gravity.
- Free fall: Motion during which the only force acting on an object is the gravitational force.
- Acceleration due to gravity (g): Near the surface of the Earth, g = 9.8 m·s⁻² downwards.
- Equations of Motion:
  - v_f = v_i + aΔt
  - v_f² = v_i² + 2aΔy
  - Δy = v_iΔt + ½aΔt²
  - Δy = ((v_i + v_f) / 2)Δt
- Time Symmetry: The time taken to reach maximum height equals the time taken to fall back to the same starting level (ignoring air resistance).
- Velocity at maximum height: v = 0 m·s⁻¹.
- Graphs:
  - Position vs. Time: A parabola.
  - Velocity vs. Time: A straight line with a constant gradient equal to g (9.8 or -9.8 depending on sign convention).
  - Acceleration vs. Time: A horizontal line at 9.8 m·s⁻² or -9.8 m·s⁻².

Common Distractors / Misconceptions to use in `wrong_answers_pool`:
- Thinking acceleration is zero at the maximum height (it is always 9.8 m·s⁻² downwards).
- Using mass in kinematics equations (mass does not affect free fall acceleration).
- Forgetting to establish a consistent sign convention (e.g., upward is positive, making a = -9.8 m·s⁻², but substituting a = +9.8 m·s⁻² instead).
- Confusing displacement (Δy) with total distance traveled.
- Assuming an object dropped from a moving balloon has an initial velocity of zero (it actually shares the balloon's velocity).

SUBTOPIC 3: WORK, ENERGY AND POWER

Definitions & Principles:
- Work (W): The work done on an object by a constant force F is FΔx cosθ, where F is the magnitude of the force, Δx the magnitude of the displacement and θ the angle between the force and the displacement. Unit: Joules (J).
- Positive Work: Adds energy to the system (θ < 90°).
- Negative Work: Removes energy from the system (90° < θ ≤ 180°), e.g., friction.
- Work-Energy Theorem: The net work done on an object is equal to the change in the object's kinetic energy. (W_net = ΔE_k = E_kf - E_ki).
- Conservative Force: A force for which the work done in moving an object between two points is independent of the path taken. (Examples: Gravitational force, Elastic force in springs, Electrostatic force).
- Non-conservative Force: A force for which the work done in moving an object between two points depends on the path taken. (Examples: Friction, Air resistance, Tension, Applied forces).
- Principle of Conservation of Mechanical Energy: The total mechanical energy (sum of gravitational potential energy and kinetic energy) in an isolated system remains constant. (E_m = E_k + E_p = constant, ONLY if W_nc = 0).
- Relationship with Non-Conservative Forces: W_nc = ΔE_k + ΔE_p.
- Power (P): The rate at which work is done or energy is expended. P = W / Δt. Unit: Watts (W).
- Average Power at constant velocity: P_ave = Fv_ave.

Common Distractors / Misconceptions to use in `wrong_answers_pool`:
- Miscalculating the angle θ in W = FΔx cosθ (e.g., using the angle with the vertical instead of the horizontal displacement).
- Forgetting to square velocity when calculating kinetic energy (E_k = ½mv²).
- Stating that friction is a conservative force.
- Ignoring the work done by gravity when calculating W_net on an incline.
- Using mass (kg) instead of weight (mg) when calculating work done by gravity.
- Confusing the Work-Energy theorem (W_net = ΔE_k) with Conservation of Mechanical Energy (which only applies when W_nc = 0).

Question: A 1200 kg car accelerates from rest. The engine delivers a constant average power of 45 kW over 8 seconds. If the work done by friction during this time is 60 000 J, what is the final velocity of the car?

W_net = W_engine + W_friction (where W_friction is negative).
