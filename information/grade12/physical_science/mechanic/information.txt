SUBTOPIC 1: NEWTON'S LAWS OF MOTION (Grade 11 Revision integrated into Grade 12)
Definitions & Principles:
- Normal Force (N): The force or the component of a force which a surface exerts on an object with which it is in contact, and which is perpendicular to the surface.
- Frictional Force (f): The force that opposes the motion of an object and which acts parallel to the surface.
- Static Friction (f_s): The force that counteracts the tendency of movement of a stationary object relative to a surface. Maximum static friction is f_s(max) = \mu_s N.
- Kinetic Friction (f_k): The force that counteracts the motion of an object relative to a surface. f_k = \mu_k N.
- Newton's First Law: A body will remain in its state of rest or motion at constant velocity unless a non-zero resultant/net force acts on it.
- Inertia: The resistance of an object to any change in its state of motion. The mass of an object is a quantitative measure of its inertia.
- Newton's Second Law: When a resultant/net force acts on an object, the object will accelerate in the direction of the force at an acceleration directly proportional to the force and inversely proportional to the mass of the object. (F_net = ma).
- Newton's Third Law: When object A exerts a force on object B, object B SIMULTANEOUSLY exerts an oppositely directed force of equal magnitude on object A.
- Newton's Law of Universal Gravitation: Each body in the universe attracts every other body with a force that is directly proportional to the product of their masses and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between their centres. (F = G m1 m2 / r^2).
- Weight: The gravitational force the Earth exerts on any object on or near its surface.

Common Distractors / Misconceptions:
- Confusing mass and weight.
- Incorrectly resolving forces on an inclined plane (e.g., using sin instead of cos for the normal force).
- Forgetting that normal force is not always equal to weight (e.g., when a force is applied at an angle).
- Believing that a constant velocity requires a constant net force (violates Newton's First Law).
- Action-reaction pairs acting on the same object instead of different objects.

SUBTOPIC 2: MOMENTUM AND IMPULSE

Definitions & Principles:
- Momentum (p): The product of an object's mass and its velocity. (p = mv). It is a vector quantity. Unit: kg·m·s⁻¹
- Newton's Second Law in terms of Momentum: The net (or resultant) force acting on an object is equal to the rate of change of momentum of the object in the direction of the net force. (F_net = Δp / Δt).
- Impulse (J): The product of the net force acting on an object and the time the net force acts on the object. (J = F_net * Δt = Δp). Unit: N·s or kg·m·s⁻¹.
- Isolated System: A system on which the net external force is zero.
- Principle of Conservation of Linear Momentum: The total linear momentum of an isolated system remains constant (is conserved) in magnitude and direction. (Σp_initial = Σp_final).
- Elastic Collision: A collision in which both total momentum and total kinetic energy are conserved. (ΣE_k_initial = ΣE_k_final).
- Inelastic Collision: A collision during which kinetic energy is not conserved (some kinetic energy is converted to heat, sound, or deformation). Momentum is still conserved.

Common Distractors / Misconceptions:
- Defining momentum as a scalar instead of a vector.
- Confusing the units of impulse (N·s) with Force (N) or Work (J).
- In calculations, failing to assign a negative sign to a change in direction (e.g., bouncing balls).
- Assuming ALL collisions conserve kinetic energy.
- Forgetting to add masses together in perfectly inelastic collisions.

SUBTOPIC 3: VERTICAL PROJECTILE MOTION IN 1D

Definitions & Principles:
- Projectile: An object upon which the only force acting is the force of gravity.
- Free fall: Motion during which the only force acting on an object is the gravitational force.
- Acceleration due to gravity (g): Near the surface of the Earth, g = 9.8 m·s⁻² downwards.
- Equations of Motion:
  - v_f = v_i + aΔt
  - v_f² = v_i² + 2aΔy
  - Δy = v_iΔt + ½aΔt²
  - Δy = ((v_i + v_f) / 2)Δt
- Time Symmetry: The time taken to reach maximum height equals the time taken to fall back to the same starting level (ignoring air resistance).
- Velocity at maximum height: v = 0 m·s⁻¹.
- Graphs:
  - Position vs. Time: A parabola.
  - Velocity vs. Time: A straight line with a constant gradient equal to g (9.8 or -9.8 depending on sign convention).
  - Acceleration vs. Time: A horizontal line at 9.8 m·s⁻² or -9.8 m·s⁻².

Common Distractors / Misconceptions:
- Thinking acceleration is zero at the maximum height (it is always 9.8 m·s⁻² downwards).
- Using mass in kinematics equations (mass does not affect free fall acceleration).
- Forgetting to establish a consistent sign convention (e.g., upward is positive, making a = -9.8 m·s⁻², but substituting a = +9.8 m·s⁻² instead).
- Confusing displacement (Δy) with total distance traveled.
- Assuming an object dropped from a moving balloon has an initial velocity of zero (it actually shares the balloon's velocity).
